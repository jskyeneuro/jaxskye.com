Research Project

Cerebellar Mutism Syndrome

Anatomical underpinnings of a postoperative syndrome following pediatric cerebellar tumor resection

Aims

Cerebellar mutism syndrome (CMS) is a debilitating postoperative complication affecting approximately 25% of pediatric patients who undergo cerebellar tumor resection. It is characterized by emotional lability, executive dysfunction, and language deficits, and is associated with worse long-term cognitive outcomes. Prior work by our group identified damage to the cerebellar outflow pathway (the deep cerebellar nuclei and superior cerebellar peduncles) as a key risk factor. This project sought to test the replicability and generalizability of those findings in an independent cohort.

  • Evaluate whether damage to the cerebellar outflow pathway replicates as a risk factor for CMS in an independent pediatric cohort
  • Characterize the lesion locations associated with CMS development across patients varying in age, tumor type, and surgical approach
  • Strengthen the neuroanatomical basis for CMS to inform optimal surgical planning and risk stratification

Description & Methods

56 pediatric patients ranging from five months to 14 years of age who underwent cerebellar tumor resection were included in this observational study. Surgical lesion locations were mapped and analyzed in relation to whether each patient developed CMS postoperatively. The overlap between each patient's lesion and the cerebellar outflow pathway was quantified and compared between CMS-positive and CMS-negative groups. Results were also evaluated against the lesion-symptom map derived from our group's prior cohort to assess cross-cohort consistency.

CMS-positive patients (n = 10) showed significantly greater lesion overlap with the cerebellar outflow pathway compared to CMS-negative patients (Cohen's d = 0.73, p = .05). Overlap with the previously derived CMS lesion-symptom map was also significantly greater in CMS-positive patients (Cohen's d = 1.1, p = .004). These results replicate and extend prior findings, reinforcing the cerebellar outflow pathway as a reliable anatomical risk factor for CMS and demonstrating generalizability across independent cohorts.
