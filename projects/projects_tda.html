Research Project

Topological Data Analysis of Brain Lesion Data

Applying TDA Mapper to reveal the shape of behavioral impairment in brain lesion patients and predict functional recovery

Aims

Brain lesion patients exhibit highly variable and complex patterns of behavioral impairment. Data-driven prediction of outcomes and lesion location from behavior is hindered by the curse of dimensionality — standard approaches like PCA and clustering may miss the nonlinear structure that organizes patients in high-dimensional behavior space. Topological Data Analysis (TDA), and the Mapper algorithm in particular, offers a framework for visualizing and characterizing this structure without assuming linearity or requiring strong distributional assumptions.

  • Visualize the shape of the distribution of behavioral deficits in brain lesion patients using TDA Mapper
  • Characterize the neuroanatomical correlates of patient groupings identified by the Mapper graph
  • Predict 3-month functional outcomes from the acute behavioral topology using leave-one-out ridge regression
  • Develop and release an open-source Python toolbox implementing TDA Mapper for clinical neuroscience data

Description & Methods

Two cohorts were analyzed: a unilateral stroke cohort from Washington University (n = 85) with acute behavioral data, and the Iowa Neurological Patient Registry lesion cohort (n = 255) with chronic behavioral data. The TDA Mapper algorithm was applied to each cohort's behavioral profiles by first reducing dimensionality with UMAP, clustering within overlapping cover elements, and constructing a graph where nodes represent patient groupings and edges reflect shared patients across nodes. Lesion mapping using a proportional subtraction approach identified brain lesion locations associated with each node. The acute WashU Mapper graph was used to predict 3-month functional outcomes via leave-one-out ridge regression, and predictions were benchmarked against high-dimensional, PCA, and UMAP approaches.

The Mapper graph revealed highly structured distributions of patients in high-dimensional behavior space, with nodes corresponding to interpretable clinical profiles — including patients with left motor deficits, right motor deficits, hemispatial neglect, worst walking, best recovery, and lasting pain. Mapper-based outcome prediction outperformed standard approaches for multiple chronic outcomes including Return to Normal Living Index, social function, depression, pain, and functional independence. Many behavioral profiles correlated with lesion location, though some were more strongly associated with demographic attributes.
